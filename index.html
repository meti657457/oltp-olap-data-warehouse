Introduction

Organizations produce huge amounts of data every day. Some systems are built to record this data quickly and correctly (OLTP), others to analyze it and support decisions (OLAP), and a data warehouse is the central place where historical data from many sources is stored for analysis.

1. OLTP (Online Transaction Processing)

OLTP processes daily transactions.

Example: ATM money withdrawal.

Characteristics

  • Very fast response for small, simple queries (insert, update, delete).
  • Supports many users at the same time.
  • Stores current, detailed data.
  • Highly normalized database to avoid duplication and keep accuracy.

Typical users

Bank tellers, cashiers, booking clerks, customers.

More examples

  • Online shopping orders
  • Airline ticket reservation
  • Student registration

2. OLAP (Online Analytical Processing)

OLAP analyzes large amounts of data.

Example: Analyzing yearly sales.

Characteristics

  • Handles complex queries over huge historical data.
  • Used by a small number of analysts and managers.
  • Mostly read operations (few updates).
  • Supports multidimensional analysis: roll-up, drill-down, slice, dice, pivot.

Typical users

Data analysts, managers, executives.

More examples

  • Comparing sales by region and year
  • Forecasting next quarter's demand
  • Customer behavior analysis

3. Data Warehouse

A data warehouse stores integrated historical data.

Example: A company stores sales data from many years.

Main features

  • Subject-oriented: organized around topics such as sales or customers.
  • Integrated: data from many sources is made consistent.
  • Time-variant: keeps historical data over time.
  • Non-volatile: data is not changed or deleted once loaded.

ETL

  • Extract: collect data from the source systems.
  • Transform: clean, convert and combine the data.
  • Load: store the result in the data warehouse.

Star schema

A central fact table (measurable data such as sales amount) connected to dimension tables (descriptive data such as time, product, customer, location).

Conclusion

OLTP systems record the day-to-day transactions of an organization, OLAP systems analyze large amounts of data to support decisions, and a data warehouse brings together historical data from many sources to make that analysis possible. Together they help organizations run their operations and make better decisions.
